Everest Base Camp Trek 

Overview & Guide
The Everest (Khumbu) region is home to Mt. Everest (8,848 m) and vibrant Sherpa culture. This classic trek (12–14 days on trail) leads through rhododendron forests and Sherpa villages (Namche Bazaar, Tengboche) up to Everest Base Camp (5,364 m) and panoramic Kala Patthar (5,555 m). It's traditionally done in spring (Mar–May) or autumn (Sept–Nov) when skies are clear. All trekking permits (Sagarmatha National Park, TIMS) are included in the package, so your main tasks are obtaining a Nepal tourist visa on arrival and preparing for the high altitude. Yeti Routes (an NTB-licensed operator) can help arrange visas, airport transfers and logistics – you just focus on training and packing.
Key treks in the Everest region include the Everest Base Camp (EBC) Trek (this page), the strenuous Everest Three High Passes Trek (18–20 days), which loops via Renjo La, Cho La and Kongma La, and the Gokyo Lakes Trek (~14–17 days) that diverts west to the turquoise Gokyo Lakes and Gokyo Ri (5,483 m) for unmatched mountain views. Yeti Routes offers these routes as separate packages (eg our Everest Three Passes Trek package) for trekkers who want to extend beyond the classic Base Camp itinerary.
Highlights 
Everest Base Camp trek highlights include:
Lukla Mountain Flight: The journey begins with a thrilling 30–40 minute flight from Kathmandu to Lukla (2,840 m), the “gateway” to Everest. On clear days you'll enjoy aerial views of soaring Himalayan peaks.


Sherpa Culture in Namche Bazaar: Trekking the quiet valleys, you'll reach Namche Bazaar (3,440 m), the bustling Sherpa “capital.” Namche offers shops, cafes, Everest museums and high-altitude acclimatization.


Tengboche Monastery: The monastery at Tengboche (3,860 m) is a spiritual highlight. Set against a stunning Himalayan backdrop, its morning chants and prayer flags immerse trekkers in Sherpa Buddhism.


Everest Base Camp: The ultimate goal – standing at Everest Base Camp (5,364 m), surrounded by prayer flags and the colossal Everest (8,848 m) looming overhead. Trekkers often celebrate this once-in-a-lifetime achievement with fellow hikers here.


Kala Patthar Sunrise: A pre-dawn hike up Kala Patthar (5,555 m) rewards you with the most famous view of Everest. From this point you'll see Everest, Lhotse, Ama Dablam and the Khumbu Icefall bathed in sunrise light.


Panoramic Vistas: The entire trek offers jaw-dropping panoramas of the Himalayas. You'll pass vast glaciers (eg Khumbu Glacier), snowy giants like Nuptse and Pumori, and enjoy landscapes from lush forest to high alpine. As one guide notes, Kala Patthar is “one of the best viewpoints in the Everest region”.


Each evening is spent in a mountain teahouse lodge (twin-share), sharing dinner with fellow trekkers under the glow of candlelight. Along the trail you'll cross thrilling suspension bridges, see herds of Himalayan wildlife, and feel deep in the “roof of the world.” (For visual highlights of Everest region treks like Gokyo and Kala Patthar, see the images above.)
Itinerary (Sample 14-Day Trek)
Below is a typical 14-day Everest Base Camp itinerary (customizable). Each day's hike is 4–7 hours on average, allowing time to acclimatize.
Days 1–2: Arrive Kathmandu (1,400 m), pre-trip meeting and gear check. Early next morning fly from Kathmandu to Lukla (2,840 m) for sunrise views. Begin trek with a few hours of walking through rhododendron forests to Phakding (2,650 m).


Day 3: Trek from Phakding to Namche Bazaar (3,440 m). Cross several high suspension bridges over the Dudh Kosi River. Overnight in Namche Bazaar (ideal acclimatization stop).


Day 4: Acclimatization Day in Namche. Hike to the Everest View Hotel (3,880 m) for panoramic Everest vistas, then return to Namche. This rest day (and gentle side hike) helps prevent altitude sickness.


Day 5: Trek from Namche Bazaar to Tengboche (3,860 m). The trail ascends through forests, with stops at prayer wheels and chortens. Arrive at Tengboche monastery with stunning views of Everest, Lhotse and Ama Dablam in front of you.


Day 6: Trek Tengboche to Dingboche (4,410 m). You pass the village of Debuche, then climb gradually into the high alpine. Dingboche is a Sherpa farming village famous for 360° mountain views (especially Ama Dablam).


Day 7: Acclimatization Day in Dingboche. Today's optional hike goes up to Nangkartshang Peak (~5,083 m) or around the village. This “climb high, sleep low” strategy lets your body adjust before higher altitudes.


Day 8: Trek Dingboche to Lobuche (4,940 m). Today is a shorter hike. The trail zig-zags through rocky moraine, offering views of Pumori, Lingtren and Nuptse. Reach Lobuche in the afternoon – one of the last “teahouses” before base camp.


Day 9: Everest Base Camp (5,364 m) & return to Gorak Shep. Start early and hike via the Khumbu Glacier to reach Everest Base Camp around midday. Stand on the EBC rock amidst fluttering prayer flags – an unforgettable moment. After photos, descend to Gorak Shep (5,190 m) for overnight.


Day 10: Kala Patthar (5,555 m) & descent to Pheriche. Pre-dawn hike up Kala Patthar for sunrise on Everest. Then descend past Lobuche and Thukla Pass back down to Pheriche (4,280 m) for the night, enjoying thicker air and warmer temperatures.


Day 11: Trek from Pheriche back down through Tengboche and Namche, staying overnight in Namche Bazaar (3,440 m). This is a long day (often split with a rest stop in Tengboche), retracing the route and enjoying a final evening at this lively town.


Day 12: Trek from Namche to Lukla (2,840 m). Cross many suspension bridges and descend steadily through jungle (Jorsalle, Monjo, Phakding). Arrive in Lukla village by afternoon and celebrate the trek's end.


Day 13: Fly from Lukla to Kathmandu. (If weather cancels Lukla flights, Yeti Routes arranges a backup flight from Manthali/Ramechhap or charter helicopter.) Back in Kathmandu you enjoy a hot shower and farewell dinner.


Day 14: Departure day – final airport transfer. Trek ends.


This itinerary is moderate–challenging : daily trekking (4–7 hrs) on steep, rocky trails and high altitudes (up to 5,555 m). Proper acclimatization days are built in. For those wanting a shorter or faster trip, Sherpa Expedition offers an 11-day EBC trek, and Yeti Routes has 12-day or helicopter-return options.

Packing & Gear Checklist
Packing wisely is critical for comfort and safety in the Himalayas. Yeti Routes provides the heavy kit: a durable 75L duffel bag (yours to keep) and group gear like sleeping bags and down jackets (available for rent). Your job is to pack smart layers and essentials. Recommended gear includes:
Clothing: Moisture-wicking base layers (merino wool or synthetic) – avoid cotton. Insulating layers: a warm fleece or wool sweater and a down puffy jacket. Outer shell: waterproof/windproof jacket and pants. Save quick-dry shirts and pants. Warm hat (for nights) and a sun/ballcap (for daytime). Neck gaiter/buff and gloves (fleece and waterproof) are essential. Pack 3-4 pairs of thermal socks.


Sleep System: 4-season sleeping bag (comfort rated around -10°C to -15°C). These can often be rented in Kathmandu for ~$30–35. Also bring a sleeping bag liner for hygiene and a lightweight travel pillow.


Footwear: Sturdy, broken-in hiking boots with good ankle support – essential for uneven Himalayan trails. Bring 2-3 pairs of wicking socks (plus liners if prone to blisters). (Caring for your feet is key; we recommend breaking in boots weeks before the trek.)


Daypack & Accessories: 30–40L backpack (water-resistant) for daily essentials and any overnight gear. Pack rain cover. Bring trekking poles (highly recommended for steep paths and heavy descent), a headlamp with spare batteries, and at least one wide-mouth water bottle (1L or larger) plus a thermal flask.


Sun Protection: High-SPF sunscreen, lip balm, and UV-blocking sunglasses (with side protection). Sun exposure and glare from snow are intense above 3,500 m. A brimmed sunhat or buff is also useful.


Personal Items: Basic toiletries (toothbrush/paste, baby wipes, quick-dry towel, hand sanitizer). Personal first-aid kit (include altitude sickness pills if prescribed, blister bandages, pain relievers). Any personal medications.


Miscellaneous: Camera with spare batteries (cold drains batteries faster). Swiss Army knife or multi-tool. Snacks/energy bars (optional, teahouses sell them but more expensive). Power bank (electricity and charging cost ~$2–3 per charge in teahouses). Travel adapter for Nepal (type C/D/M sockets).


Cash: Carry at least USD $150-200 in small bills (or equivalent rupees) for tips, showers ($2-3), gear rental, internet usage and snacks. Note: ATM machines exist in Namche and Kathmandu, but may not always work; bring enough cash to cover all trek expenses.


The golden rule: pack as light as possible. Every ounce counts. We handle the heavy items – you focus on layering for warmth. Layering is key to stay comfortable (remove or add layers as the temperature changes). Prioritize gear for cold nights (puffy coat and insulated sleeping bag) and rain protection.
Planning Tips 
Best Time to Go: Late spring (Mar–May) and early autumn (Sept–Nov) offer the best balance of weather and clear skies. Summer brings monsoon rains (scenic valleys but slippery trails), and winter is very cold (high passes often closed).


Visas & Permits: Tourists get a Nepal visa on arrival (US$30 for 15 days)– bring 2 passport photos and cash. Yeti Routes arranges your trekking permits (TIMS and Sagarmatha NP entry) in Kathmanduso no need to queue at offices.


Fitness & Preparation: Moderate fitness is required (4–6 hours walking per day on uneven terrain). Prior hiking experience is helpful. We advise some cardio training (hiking, cycling) 1–2 months before. The two acclimatization days (Namche and Dingboche) greatly reduce altitude sickness risks. Listen to your body: headaches or nausea are warning signs. Guides will monitor blood oxygen daily. Stay well-hydrated (3–4 liters/day)and avoid alcohol during ascent.


Altitude Sickness: EBC (5,364 m) is very high. Follow “climb high, sleep low” – our itinerary includes acclimatization hikes. If symptoms occur, we stop ascending or descending. Your guide carries a pulse oximeter and can arrange helicopter evacuation if needed (hence mandatory insurance covering rescue).


Lukla Flight Delays: Flights to/from Lukla depend on weather. Yeti Routes has contingency plans: in bad weather we use road transfer to/from Manthali or arrange a helicopter flight (extra cost) to avoid delays. We update you daily on flight status.
