**Target Keyword:** "altitude sickness nepal" / "ams prevention" (500 searches/month)

**Outline:**
1. Introduction (200 words)
   - What is AMS (Acute Mountain Sickness)
   - How common it is (50% of trekkers experience mild symptoms)
   - Why Nepal trekking has higher risk
   - Importance of recognition

2. Understanding Altitude (300 words)
   - How altitude affects oxygen
     - Sea level: 100% O2
     - 3,000m: 70%
     - 5,000m: 50%
   - Altitude zones:
     - High: 2,400-3,600m (Namche)
     - Very high: 3,600-5,500m (EBC)
     - Extreme: 5,500m+ (Summits)
   - Acclimatization process
   - Why some people adapt faster

3. Three Types of Altitude Sickness (400 words)
   - **AMS (Acute Mountain Sickness) - MILD**
     - Symptoms: Headache, nausea, fatigue, dizziness
     - Onset: 6-12 hours after ascent
     - Severity: Uncomfortable but manageable
     - Treatment: Rest, hydration, Diamox

   - **HACE (High Altitude Cerebral Edema) - SERIOUS**
     - Symptoms: Severe headache, confusion, loss of coordination
     - Warning signs: Can't walk straight line (ataxia test)
     - Severity: Life-threatening if untreated
     - Treatment: IMMEDIATE DESCENT + dexamethasone

   - **HAPE (High Altitude Pulmonary Edema) - SERIOUS**
     - Symptoms: Extreme breathlessness, gurgling chest, pink frothy sputum
     - Warning signs: Can't catch breath even at rest
     - Severity: Life-threatening
     - Treatment: IMMEDIATE DESCENT + oxygen

4. Symptoms Checklist (300 words)
   - **Mild Symptoms (AMS):**
     - Headache (most common)
     - Loss of appetite
     - Nausea/vomiting
     - Fatigue beyond normal
     - Dizziness
     - Difficulty sleeping
     - Shortness of breath with exertion

   - **DANGER SIGNS (HACE/HAPE):**
     - Confusion, irrational behavior
     - Severe, persistent headache
     - Loss of coordination (ataxia)
     - Extreme breathlessness at rest
     - Gurgling/rattling in chest
     - Blue lips/fingernails (cyanosis)
     - Coughing up blood/pink sputum

5. Prevention Strategies (500 words)
   - **Acclimatization Schedule:**
     - "Climb high, sleep low"
     - Don't ascend >500m/day above 3,000m
     - Rest day every 1,000m gain
     - Example: EBC itinerary with rest days

   - **Hydration:**
     - Drink 3-4L water/day
     - Monitor urine color (should be clear)
     - Avoid alcohol/caffeine

   - **Diamox (Acetazolamide):**
     - What it does (speeds acclimatization)
     - Dosage: 125mg twice daily
     - When to start (1-2 days before ascent)
     - Side effects (tingling, frequent urination)
     - Consult doctor before trek

   - **Lifestyle on Trek:**
     - Trek at slow, steady pace ("pole pole")
     - Eat well even if not hungry
     - Avoid sleeping pills
     - Monitor symptoms daily

   - **Pre-Trek Preparation:**
     - Fitness helps but doesn't prevent AMS
     - Consider altitude pre-acclimatization (if available)
     - Medical clearance if over 60 or have conditions

6. Treatment & Response (300 words)
   - **Mild AMS Treatment:**
     - Stop ascending
     - Rest for 24-48 hours
     - Hydrate aggressively
     - Take Diamox
     - Ibuprofen for headache
     - If no improvement → descend 500m

   - **Severe AMS/HACE/HAPE Treatment:**
     - DESCEND IMMEDIATELY (golden rule)
     - Don't wait until morning
     - Descend minimum 500-1,000m
     - Oxygen if available
     - Dexamethasone for HACE
     - Nifedipine for HAPE
     - Helicopter evacuation if can't walk

   - **When to Evacuate:**
     - Symptoms worsen despite treatment
     - Can't descend on own power
     - Loss of consciousness
     - Severe HACE/HAPE symptoms

7. Common Myths Debunked (200 words)
   - **Myth:** "Fit people don't get AMS"
     - **Truth:** Fitness doesn't prevent altitude sickness

   - **Myth:** "I've done high altitude before, I'm immune"
     - **Truth:** Previous success doesn't guarantee immunity

   - **Myth:** "Diamox is cheating"
     - **Truth:** It's a legitimate preventive medication

   - **Myth:** "Headache at altitude is normal"
     - **Truth:** ANY headache is AMS until proven otherwise

8. FAQ Section
   - What percentage of trekkers get altitude sickness?
   - Can you die from altitude sickness?
